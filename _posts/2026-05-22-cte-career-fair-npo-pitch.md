    <h1>NPO Pitch: Unified Esports League</h1>
    <p class="subtitle">Building an inclusive esports platform for the IDD community</p>
    <span class="date-badge">CTE Career Fair &mdash; May 22, 2026</span>

  <div class="section-card">
    <h2>The Opportunity</h2>
    <p>
      On <strong>May 22nd, 2026</strong>, I had the chance to pitch a real product to a panel of industry professionals at the <strong>CTE Career Fair</strong> — an event organized by the Career and Technical Education program and my computer science teacher, <strong>Mr. Mortensen</strong>.
    </p>
    <p>
      It was one of the most grounding experiences I've had as a student developer: putting months of work in front of people who build software for a living and asking them to tear it apart.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <h2>The Organization: Unified Esports League</h2>
    <p>
      The nonprofit I pitched to was the <strong>Unified Esports League (UESL)</strong> — an organization dedicated to making esports accessible to <em>everyone</em>, including individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD). Their mission centers on inclusion, and my goal was to build something that actually lived up to that.
    </p>
    <p>
      My team designed a <strong>community web platform</strong> for UESL members — a space where people could discover resources, share their experiences, and connect with others without being left out.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <h2>The Gamification Feature</h2>
    <p>
      The section I'm most proud of — and the one that sparked the most conversation with the panel — was the <strong>Gamification System</strong>. Rather than a passive resource hub, we wanted the website to feel like something you <em>play</em>.
    </p>
    <div class="feature-grid">
      <div class="feature-item">
        <span class="icon"></span>
        <div class="text">
          <strong>Create Your Own Game</strong>
          Design and customize games to share with the community
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="feature-item">
        <span class="icon"></span>
        <div class="text">
          <strong>Compete & Collaborate</strong>
          Play with other UESL members in real time
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="feature-item">
        <span class="icon"></span>
        <div class="text">
          <strong>Achievements & Progress</strong>
          Earn rewards and track your journey on the platform
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <p style="margin-top:1.2rem;">
      The idea was to put game creation in the hands of the users themselves. Someone with an IDD shouldn't just be a spectator in esports — they should be able to <strong>build the games, set the rules, and invite others to play</strong>. It closes the gap between "user" and "creator" in a meaningful way.
    </p>
    <p>
      Building this feature meant thinking carefully about <strong>accessibility at every layer</strong> — from UI controls down to how we structured game state — ensuring the experience was navigable without relying solely on mouse precision.
    </p>
  </div>

  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <h2>What We Heard</h2>
    <p>The panel's response was encouraging and direct. Their feedback included:</p>
    <ul class="feedback-list">
      <li>
        <span class="tag">Features</span>
        Suggestions around social matchmaking and how players discover each other's games
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="tag">Bugs</span>
        A couple of edge cases in the game creation flow that we hadn't caught in testing
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="tag">Pitch</span>
        Lead with user impact before diving into technical implementation
      </li>
    </ul>
    <p style="margin-top:1rem;">The constructive criticism landed well. It wasn't discouraging — it felt like being taken seriously.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <h2>Takeaways</h2>
    <p>
      This experience pushed me to think about software not just as code, but as something people actually live inside. The IDD community doesn't need a platform built <em>for</em> them — they need one built <em>with</em> them in mind from the ground up.
    </p>
    <p>
      I came out of the pitch more motivated to refine the gamification system, address the flagged bugs, and continue developing the UESL platform into something the community can genuinely use.
    </p>
    <div class="quote-block">
      Inclusivity in esports isn't a feature — it's the foundation.
    </div>
